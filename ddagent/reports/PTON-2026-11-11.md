# Due diligence: PELOTON INTERACTIVE, INC. (PTON)

CIK 1639825 · 10-K filed 2026-08-06 · accession 0001639825-26-000038

**Overall risk: MEDIUM.** Peloton's first annual profit ($63.2M), rising cash ($1.21B), lower debt and effective internal controls are positives. They are offset by two years of falling revenue, liabilities above assets, $1.33B of debt, and ongoing recall-related litigation, including a new Bike+ recall. The 10-K discloses no going concern doubt, no current material weakness, no restatement and no auditor change (EY remains the auditor). Past material weaknesses (FY21–FY24) were remediated.

## Financial flags

- **Medium:** Revenue has declined two years running: $2.70B (FY24) to $2.49B (FY25) to $2.45B (FY26). Why: Profitability has been achieved through cost cuts rather than top-line growth, which may limit sustainability.
- **Medium:** Total liabilities ($2.196B) exceed total assets ($2.056B), a stockholders' deficit of roughly $140M (derived), though the gap has narrowed from FY25. Why: Thin equity cushion; reliance on debt financing.
- **Medium:** Long-term debt of $1.33B against $1.21B cash; the first profit ($63.2M) is small relative to interest expense cited in the 10-K ($123.8M, one of the periods reported). Why: Interest burden is heavy relative to earnings; profits are thin.
- **Low:** Cash rose from $697.6M to $1.21B over two years and debt fell from $1.55B to $1.33B. Why: Liquidity and deleveraging are improving, which is a positive.

## Risks from the 10-K

- **Medium · financial:** Profitability is new and may not be sustained; the company has a history of operating losses. "Although we reported net income in fiscal year 2026, we have incurred significant operating losses in prior periods and may not be able to sustain profitability" [1A-p004]
- **Medium · financial:** Credit facility covenants include a minimum liquidity of $250M and minimum Subscription Revenue of $1.2B (when revolver is drawn). Total debt is about $1.3B, including $350M of 5.50% convertible notes due 2029. "requires us, when revolving loans are outstanding, to maintain a total level of liquidity of not less than $250.0 million" [1A-p175]
- **High · legal:** Ongoing class action, personal injury, and regulatory proceedings tied to Tread+ and Bike recalls. A new Bike+ recall was announced in November 2025. "We have been and in some cases continue to be subject to class action litigation, private personal injury claims and other regulatory proceedings" [1A-p203]
- **Medium · operational:** Product recalls (Tread+ 2021, Bike 2023, Bike+ Nov 2025) may bring further material costs, and insurance may not cover them. "in November 2025, in collaboration with the CPSC and Health Canada, we announced a voluntary recall of certain Original Series Bike+ units" [1A-p202]
- **Medium · operational:** Sole reliance on contract manufacturers, often single-source, concentrated in Taiwan, China and Thailand, plus tariff exposure. "We are solely reliant on contract manufacturers for all of our manufacturing needs." [1A-p054]
- **Medium · governance:** The company previously had material weaknesses (FY21–FY24). They are now remediated, but could recur. Risks are flagged around last-mile partners and inventory counts. "we identified certain material weaknesses in our internal control over financial reporting." [1A-p178]
- **Medium · operational:** Demand forecasting and inventory risk have led to write-downs and disputes with suppliers over purchase commitments. "We have in recent periods experienced, and may continue to experience decreases in consumer demand, resulting in inventory write-downs" [1A-p020]

## Red flags

- Going concern: not found.
- Material weakness: not found.
- Restatement: not found.
- Auditor change: not found.
- **Major litigation: found.** "We have been and in some cases continue to be subject to class action litigation, private personal injury claims and other regulatory proceedings" [1A-p203]

## Open questions for a reviewer

- Read Note 12 for the size of recall-related litigation accruals and the status of class actions and CPSC matters, including the Nov 2025 Bike+ recall costs.
- Is the FY26 profit driven by cost cuts or one-time items? Check operating income and free cash flow.
- What are the trends in subscriptions and churn behind the revenue decline?
- Check the covenant headroom on the $1.2B subscription revenue test and how the company plans to refinance the 2029 maturities.
- Confirm the stockholders' deficit and the quality of the remediation of earlier material weaknesses, including controls over last-mile partners and shared services.
