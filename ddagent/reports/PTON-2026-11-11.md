# Due diligence: PELOTON INTERACTIVE, INC. (PTON)

CIK 1639825 · 10-K filed 2026-08-06 · accession 0001639825-26-000038

**Overall risk: MEDIUM.** Peloton reached net income of $63.2M in FY26 and cash rose to $1,206.6M. Liabilities fell, and management reports effective internal controls (9A-p005) with an Ernst & Young attestation. No going-concern language was found. Offsetting this, revenue has fallen two years running, and liabilities still exceed assets. About $1.3B of debt carries covenants and high interest costs. Recall-related class actions and injury claims continue. Material weaknesses existed through FY2024, though the 10-K describes them as previously identified. Rating: medium. The material_weakness flag is marked not_found because the weaknesses were remediated and FY26 controls are effective. Research only, not investment advice.

## Financial flags

- **Medium:** Revenue has declined two years in a row: $2,700.5M (FY24), $2,490.8M (FY25), $2,446.0M (FY26). Why: Profitability was reached on shrinking sales, so it relies on cost cuts rather than growth.
- **Medium:** Total liabilities ($2,195.6M) exceed total assets ($2,055.9M) at FY26, a deficit of about $139.7M (my calculation). The gap has narrowed from FY25. Why: Negative book equity limits the balance-sheet cushion.
- **Medium:** Long-term debt is $1,330.0M against cash of $1,206.6M. Interest expense is cited at $123.8M and $134.5M in two periods. Why: Net debt is modest, but interest costs are heavy relative to FY26 net income of $63.2M.
- **Low:** The turn to profit is recent: net income was $63.2M in FY26 after losses of $118.9M (FY25) and $551.9M (FY24). Why: One profitable year is a short record. Sustainability is not yet proven.

## Risks from the 10-K

- **Medium · financial:** The Term Loan and revolver carry covenants. When the revolver is drawn, liquidity must be at least $250M and trailing subscription revenue at least $1.2B. Total indebtedness is about $1.3B, including $350M of 5.50% convertible notes due 2029. "requires us, when revolving loans are outstanding, to maintain a total level of liquidity of not less than $250.0 million" [1A-p175]
- **High · legal:** The company faces ongoing class actions, personal injury claims and government inquiries tied to the Tread+ and Bike recalls. A further recall of Original Series Bike+ units was announced in November 2025. "We have been and in some cases continue to be subject to class action litigation, private personal injury claims" [1A-p203]
- **Medium · operational:** Profitability may not be sustained. The company has a history of operating losses, and subscriber retention and demand are uncertain. "Although we reported net income in fiscal year 2026, we have incurred significant operating losses in prior periods" [1A-p004]
- **Medium · governance:** Material weaknesses were identified in internal controls in FY2021 to FY2024. Controls are now reported effective, but the risk of recurrence is flagged, including in physical inventory verification at last-mile partners. "we identified certain material weaknesses in our internal control over financial reporting." [1A-p178]
- **Medium · operational:** Demand forecasting and inventory risk. Past demand declines led to write-downs, discounting and disputes with suppliers over purchase commitments. "We have in recent periods experienced, and may continue to experience decreases in consumer demand, resulting in inventory write-downs" [1A-p020]
- **Medium · operational:** The company relies on contract manufacturers, often a single supplier. Their facilities are concentrated in Taiwan, China and Thailand, which adds supply-chain and tariff exposure. "In most cases, we rely on only a single supplier for our products and some critical components." [1A-p054]

## Open questions for a reviewer

- Review Note 12 (Commitments and Contingencies) for the size of the recall litigation exposure and any accruals.
- Check the subscription revenue trend against the $1.2B trailing covenant minimum and the headroom on it.
- Look at what drove the FY26 profit, including cost cuts, restructuring and one-time items, and whether margins are sustainable.
- Check the maturity and refinancing plan for the Term Loan and notes (2029) and the dilution from the convertible notes.
- Confirm that the material weaknesses are fully remediated and that the shared-services transition has not weakened controls.
- Confirm there was no auditor change in recent years (none was found in the 10-K text searched).
