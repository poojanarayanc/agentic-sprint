# Due diligence: PELOTON INTERACTIVE, INC. (PTON)

CIK 1639825 · 10-K filed 2026-08-06 · accession 0001639825-26-000038

**Overall risk: MEDIUM.** Peloton has improved: FY2026 net income was 63.2M, cash was 1.2066B and debt fell. Management concluded ICFR was effective (9A-p005), and no going-concern language turned up in the searches. Offsetting this: revenue has fallen two years running, liabilities exceed assets, about 1.3B of debt carries covenants, there is continuing product-safety litigation and recall exposure, and material weaknesses were reported in FY2021–FY2024. The material weakness flag is marked not_found because the weaknesses are described as previously identified and remediated. This is research, not investment advice.

## Financial flags

- **Medium:** Revenue has declined two years in a row (2.70B → 2.49B → 2.446B). Why: Profitability was reached through cost cuts rather than growth; the top line is still shrinking.
- **Medium:** Total liabilities (2.196B) exceed total assets (2.056B) at FY2026 year-end. Why: Implies a stockholders' deficit, which limits balance sheet cushion, though the gap narrowed from FY2025 (2.539B liabilities vs 2.125B assets).
- **Medium:** Long-term debt of 1.33B against cash of 1.2066B; interest expense was cited at 123.8M and 134.5M in two periods. Why: Heavy fixed interest burden relative to the thin net income of 63.2M.
- **Low:** Positive turnaround: net income of 63.2M in FY2026 versus losses of 118.9M and 551.9M; cash up from 697.6M to 1.2066B over two years; debt down from 1.549B. Why: The trend is improving, but one profitable year is a short track record.

## Risks from the 10-K

- **Medium · financial:** Profitability is new and may not be sustained; the company has a history of large operating losses. "Although we reported net income in fiscal year 2026, we have incurred significant operating losses in prior periods" [1A-p004]
- **High · financial:** About $1.3B of debt (Term Loan plus $350M 5.50% convertible notes due 2029). Credit agreement covenants include a $1.2B subscription revenue test and $250M minimum liquidity when the revolver is drawn. "to maintain Subscription Revenue of the company and its subsidiaries of at least $1.2 billion for the four-quarter trailing period" [1A-p175]
- **High · legal:** Ongoing class actions, personal injury claims and government inquiries tied to Tread+ and Bike recalls. A new Bike+ recall was announced in November 2025. "In November 2025, in collaboration with the CPSC and Health Canada, we announced a voluntary recall of certain Original Series Bike+ units" [1A-p202]
- **Medium · legal:** The company has been named in securities class actions and stockholder derivative suits. "We have been named in securities class action lawsuits and stockholder derivative actions" [1A-p309]
- **Medium · governance:** Material weaknesses were identified in FY2021–FY2024. They are described as previously identified and remediated, but could recur, for example around inventory counts with last-mile partners. "we identified certain material weaknesses in our internal control over financial reporting" [1A-p178]
- **Medium · operational:** Demand forecasting and inventory risk: past write-downs, discounting and disputes with suppliers over purchase commitments. "We have in recent periods experienced, and may continue to experience decreases in consumer demand, resulting in inventory write-downs" [1A-p020]
- **Medium · operational:** Sole reliance on contract manufacturers, often a single supplier, concentrated in Taiwan, China and Thailand. Tariffs are also a risk. "We are solely reliant on contract manufacturers for all of our manufacturing needs." [1A-p054]

## Open questions for a reviewer

- Read Note 12 (Commitments and Contingencies) for the size of accruals and potential exposure from the Tread+/Bike/Bike+ recall litigation and the securities class actions.
- Confirm that the earlier material weaknesses are fully remediated, and check how the shift to a shared-services provider (9A-p008) affects controls.
- Check the quality of FY2026 earnings: restructuring, impairment and stock-based compensation effects, and free cash flow.
- Review the subscription revenue trend against the $1.2B covenant threshold and the 2029 debt maturity wall.
- Confirm that the auditor (Ernst & Young) has not changed. No change was found in the sections searched, but Item 9 was not reviewed.
